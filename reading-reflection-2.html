AI, Learning, and the Importance of Using Our Brains

After seeing the web page and video, I found out that people could use artificial intelligence, also known as AI, in everyday life in ways that don’t “handicap” them. Before I saw AI chat bots and thought that people would use AI like a genie that would magically do everything for them. Both the video and webpage made me realize that convenience like this can come with consequences. The biggest lesson I took away from this is that AI itself is not the problem, the real problem is the way that we use it. The MIT study was interesting because researchers compared people who wrote essays using a search engine, with a group that weren’t allowed to use it. The “Brain only” group showed the strongest and widest neural connectivity, while the search engine group showed the weakest overall connectivity. People using a search engine had more trouble remembering what they had wrote and reported feeling less control over the creativity of their essays. This made me think about the difference between getting an assignment over with versus pacing yourself, learning and making the essay. If AI writes most of an assignment for students, they may submit something that sounds good, but that they might not understand and won’t have a lasting impact. The discussion in the first one I saw goes more in depth on this. One thing that stood out to me was the idea that people should use AI to improve their thinking instead of replacing it, this is an important difference. For example, instead of asking AI to write an entire essay, people could write a draft of the essay and then ask AI to critique the paper, look for weaknesses, or ask questions about it. That would help people grow in their writing while having their voice preserved. I also found the discussion about struggle intriguing. Trying to remember information, solve difficult problems, making mistakes, and rewriting something are important parts of learning how to write. If AI quickly takes away every troublesome task, people might not learn skills like critical thinking, creativity, communication, and problem-solving that happen when writing. At the same time, I do not think these prove that AI right away makes people less intelligent or causes dementia. The MIT researchers describe their findings as preliminary, explain that their study had a limited number of participants, and note that their results were focused specifically on educational essay writing. The study was also not peer-reviewed when the info was given. This is important because some statements in the interview sound much more certain than the actual research allows. If I was using AI, these wouldn’t convince me to stop using AI. Instead, they would have made me think that I need to use it more responsibly. AI can be extremely useful for people, but I personally want to learn and have my own voice and opinion, and I don’t just want to simply teach me how to let technology think for me.
