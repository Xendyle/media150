Reading Reflection 1

Reading + reflection 1: “In Plato’s Cave” chapter 1 from On Photography

In the text, Plato's Cave, Sontag pushes the idea that photographs and pictures are more than simple preservations of the past, or the history of the world that we live in. Reading this essay made me think differently about my own relationship with photography and images in general. In the place were we live, there are cameras on phone, taking pictures has become a daily habit (I've know to take my fair share of selfies). And through all of this Sontag shows us that every photograph represents a choice and a perspective rather than a completely objective truth. I use my camera mostly as a way to save memories or experiences, or to save important information. When I am taking pictures with friends, of places I visit, or something as simple as homework and documents to save and upload, I notice throughout the day, pictures help me take a moment to step back and pay attention to my surroundings. At the same time, I realize that every image I capture is selective. I decide what enters the frame, what is excluded, and the moment that I choose becomes frozen in time. Because of these choices, photos are not just evidence of the past but interpretations of it and quite literally the eyes of which we see things. Making images through photos and graphic design also lets people, including myself, to express themselves as well as their ideas that may often be difficult to tell or show others with just words alone. Images have the power to make the audience feel things mentally and physically even to the point of telling stories, and even making deep concepts visible and able to grasp. If I were to show our world at this moment using photos or illustrations, I would do the same as I would if I was writing (I am a writer). I would focus on themes of community and isolation, I would use colors and things rather than words to show it. In this day and age, especially with the technology we have at our disposal today, even though it lets people connect to each other instantly, it can still just as well disconnect people. A visual project could show people surrounded by screens. Or people could be together but someone would still feel alone, people physically together but absorbed in their devices or on each other. We could also focus on moments of genuine or superficial human interaction. Through photography, I could bring public spaces to other places where people can relate. Through illustration, I could also put digital symbols like notifications, as well as use colors to further show the disconnect between the real world, people and assertiveness. And further show scenes to emphasize how technology shapes our daily experiences. Through this semester, I hope to see each project not as work but as part of a bigger work. By using tools that I can make a clearer artistic voice and explore themes that matter to me.
