Reflection on Heela: “My Stolen Future” (Motherhood in Crisis)

In Heela’s story, “My Stolen Future,” drawing has a big part in helping readers and people understand the emotional and social impact of teenage pregnancy particularly in Sierra Leone. This story has text, photographs, and a mix of many visuals. The illustrations help create a unique visual experience that lets readers connect more deeply with Heela’s personal thoughts, fears, and memories. The scenes highlight emotions and experiences that would be very hard to understand by just reading it like a memoir book or text. For example, it shows times of isolation, anxiety, and the loss of the opportunity of education symbolically, helping readers imagine what the main character in this story, Heela, felt rather than just stating the facts of what happened to her. The illustrations differ significantly from the videos. The photographs give a documentary styletype of evidence of real people, places, and living conditions. They create credibility and remind readers that these events that are described are in fact real. But the photographs and videos are stuck or trapped to specific moments captured by a camera lens and batteries. Drawings, on the other hand, can show memories, emotions, dreams, and experiences that were never taken by a camera. They often use color, composition, and visual symbolism to represent feelings such as fear, sadness, or hope. In Heela’s story, the illustrations create a more intimate view of her experiences as a young girl whose future changed because of pregnancy. The visual style also softens some of the harsh realities while still having the seriousness, making the story accessible to a wider audience. Compared with just text alone, drawings help the readers be deeper in emotionally in the story and help visualize situations that might feel distant or abstract if it was just written down. A written version of emotional struggles would force readers to imagine the scene for themselves, but drawings automatically give people an image so that they now have a visual context right on the paper. This can help increase emotional understanding that goes beyond the barrier of language. Images also take complex social issues and make them easier to understand by putting together words and imagery in a way that guides the reader through the story. When I compare the photos, the drawings give a bigger creative freedom. They can show many things that happened in the past, private moments, or even emotional states that can't be seen through a camera lens, especially in remote places. These drawings can also protect the privacy as well as the dignity of vulnerable people in these situations by avoiding direct outing while still bringing awareness by telling their stories, especially in a story about teenage pregnancy, trauma, and hardship. The drawings let readers to focus on Heela’s humanity and emotions rather than viewing her only as a subject of a news report. The use of many visuals in “My Stolen Future” strengthens the storytelling by having emotional depth and visual symbolism. Together with text, photography, and video it creates a better and more engaging story. They help readers understand not only the facts of Heela’s experience but also the emotional reality of what it means to lose opportunities and be a mother at a young age. This combination of media makes the story more powerful and memorable than text or photography alone.
